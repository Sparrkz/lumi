Exactly — **this is where “My Paths” becomes really powerful.**

If someone says:

> **“I want to learn reasoning.”**

There isn't necessarily a conventional **subject** called "Reasoning" that should dictate the whole experience.

Lumi could turn that intention into a path:

### My Path

**Think More Clearly**
*Build stronger reasoning and problem-solving skills*

**Path**

1. Understanding arguments
2. Identifying assumptions
3. Recognizing patterns
4. Logical reasoning
5. Evaluating evidence
6. Solving unfamiliar problems
7. Applying reasoning to real situations

And Lumi doesn't have to assume the learner wants *academic logic*.

It could first figure out what they mean:

> **What kind of reasoning are you interested in?**
>
> 🧩 Solving problems
> 🔎 Thinking critically
> 🧠 Logical reasoning
> 💬 Building better arguments
> 🌍 Making better everyday decisions
> **A bit of everything**

Then the path adapts.

---

### This is why I wouldn't structure Lumi as:

**Subject → Course → Lessons**

I'd structure it more like:

**Intent → Path → Experiences → Growth**

Someone can enter Lumi saying:

> "I want to learn reasoning."

Another says:

> "I want to become better at communicating."

Another:

> "I want to understand money."

Another:

> "I need to pass JAMB."

Another:

> "I want to learn Python."

Those are fundamentally different **intentions**, but Lumi can represent all of them as **paths**.

And some paths don't even need a traditional "finish line."

For reasoning, for example, Lumi could eventually say:

> **You're becoming better at evaluating claims.**
>
> You've practiced distinguishing evidence from assumptions across 12 different situations.

That's much more interesting than:

> **Reasoning Course — 63% complete**

So yes, **“My Paths” should probably become a core concept in Lumi's architecture.** It gives you a way to support *anything someone genuinely wants to learn*, without forcing everything into school subjects.
