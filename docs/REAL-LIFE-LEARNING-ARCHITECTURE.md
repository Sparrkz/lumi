---

# 1. Purpose

Lumi is not an LMS.

It should not be limited to:

* school subjects
* exams
* courses
* certificates
* classrooms

Lumi should support **learning as a lifelong human activity**.

A person should be able to open Lumi at age 7 and learn about dinosaurs.

At 17, they might use it to learn programming.

At 25, they might learn entrepreneurship.

At 40, they might learn photography.

At 70, they might learn a new language.

The underlying system remains the same:

> **A person wants to understand, improve, create, solve, or accomplish something. Lumi helps them get there.**

---

# 2. Core Principle

Lumi should connect:

```text
LEARNING
   ↓
UNDERSTANDING
   ↓
PRACTICE
   ↓
REAL LIFE
   ↓
CAPABILITY
```

The ultimate purpose of learning is not merely to know something.

It is to make the learner more capable.

---

# 3. Real-Life Learning Definition

Real-life learning is any learning experience where knowledge or skills are connected to meaningful activity outside traditional academic instruction.

Examples:

* managing money
* cooking
* repairing something
* communicating
* negotiating
* writing
* creating
* building
* organizing
* researching
* solving problems
* using technology
* making decisions
* navigating unfamiliar situations
* developing a career
* pursuing a hobby

---

Traditional education often starts with:

> "Today we're learning percentages."

Lumi can start with:

> "You want to know how much you'll save when something is 20% off."

# 10. Learn

Direct acquisition of knowledge.

Example:

> Understanding how interest works.

---

# 11. Practice

Repeated application in controlled situations.

Example:

> Calculating interest on example transactions.

---

# 12. Build

Creating something tangible.

Example:

> Build a personal budget spreadsheet.

---

# 13. Create

Producing something expressive.

Example:

> Write a short story.

---

# 14. Solve

Applying knowledge to a problem.

Example:

> Determine why a small business is losing money.

---

# 15. Explore

Open-ended discovery.

Example:

> "Why do stars have different colors?"

---

# 16. Observe

Learning through noticing.

Example:

> Identify examples of symmetry around you.

---

# 17. Experiment

Test ideas.

Example:

> Change one variable and observe what happens.

---

# 18. Discuss

Learning through conversation.

Example:

> Debate the advantages and disadvantages of remote work.

---

# 19. Reflect

Turn experience into understanding.

Example:

> "What worked? What didn't?"

---

# 20. Teach

One of the strongest forms of demonstrating understanding.

Lumi can allow a learner to explain a concept to another person or to an AI.

# 22. Scenario-Based Learning

Lumi presents a realistic situation.

Example:

> "You're planning a small event with a limited budget."

The learner must make decisions.

Lumi evaluates reasoning rather than just memorization.

---

# 23. Simulation

The learner interacts with a simplified model of reality.

Examples:

* business simulation
* budgeting simulation
* ecosystem simulation
* physics simulation
* negotiation simulation

---

# 24. Project Learning

The learner creates something substantial.

Example:

```text
Goal:
Build a website

Project:
Personal portfolio

Skills:
HTML
CSS
JavaScript
Design
Deployment
```

---

# 25. Field Tasks

Lumi can ask learners to do something in their environment.

Example:

> "Find three examples of geometric shapes around you."

The learner can optionally submit evidence.

---

# 26. Observation-Based Learning

The world becomes the classroom.

Example:

> "Observe how people move through a public space and identify patterns."

This can teach:

* design
* mathematics
* sociology
* architecture
* research

depending on context.

---

# 28. Real-Life Evidence

Evidence may include:

* photo
* video
* written response
* uploaded artifact
* project
* explanation
* observation
* reflection
* performance

---

# 29. Evidence Must Be Appropriate

Not every activity needs evidence upload.

Lumi should avoid unnecessary friction.

Sometimes the best evidence is simply:

> A successful interaction.

---

# 30. Real-Life Learning Is Not Surveillance

Lumi should not require learners to constantly prove what they are doing.

Participation should be intentional.

---

# 31. Privacy

Real-world learning can involve:

* locations
* images
* voices
* documents
* other people

Lumi must minimize collection and clearly communicate what is being stored.

---

# 32. Children

Children require stronger privacy and safety protections.

Real-life activities for children should be:

* age appropriate
* safe
* understandable
* supervised where necessary

Lumi should never encourage children to perform unsafe tasks merely to generate learning evidence.

---

# 33. Accessibility

Real-life activities must have alternatives.

Example:

If an activity says:

> "Go outside and observe three plants."

An alternative might be:

> "Use provided images or observations from a safe indoor environment."

The learning objective should remain intact where possible.

---

# 34. Neurodivergent Learners

Lumi should avoid assuming one "normal" way to complete an activity.

Where the objective allows flexibility, learners can choose:

```text
Record
```

# 40. Offline Learning

# 43. Contextual Suggestions

# 44. Just-in-Time Learning

A person encounters a problem.

They ask Lumi.

Lumi teaches exactly what is needed.

Example:

> "How do I calculate this percentage?"

Lumi explains the relevant concept.

Then:

> "Want to try another one?"

Lumi should support natural questions:

> "Why is my Wi-Fi slow?"

> "How does compound interest work?"

> "How do I structure a presentation?"

> "Why does my code crash?"

The system determines whether the interaction should become:

* explanation
* diagnostic
* practice
* project
* journey

# 47. Avoid Over-Teaching

If the learner simply needs an answer, Lumi should not force them through a course.

The system should distinguish:

> **Information request**

from:

> **Learning intention**

# 54. Real-Life Mastery

A learner should eventually demonstrate:

> **"I can use this without Lumi."**

That is an important measure of success.

---

# 55. Independence

Lumi should gradually reduce scaffolding.

```text
Heavy support
 ↓
Moderate support
 ↓
Light support
 ↓
Independent performance
```

---

# 56. Support Fading

This is essential.

The AI should not become a permanent crutch.

The goal is:

> **Increasing learner independence.**

# 57. Real-Life Mistakes

Lumi should treat mistakes as learning evidence.

If a learner makes an incorrect decision in a simulation:

```text

 ↓
Outcome
 ↓
Reflection
 ↓
Explanation
 ↓
Retry
```

---

# 58. Consequence-Free Practice

Simulations allow learners to practice decisions without real-world harm.

This is especially valuable for:

* financial concepts
* business
* communication
* technical systems
* planning

---

# 59. Safety Boundaries

Lumi should never turn dangerous real-world activities into "learning challenges."

For safety-sensitive topics, it should use:

* simulations
* supervised educational contexts
* safe alternatives
* informational explanations

when appropriate.

> "What was your favorite part?"

> "Which assumption influenced your decision?"

> Understanding saving.

> Budgeting and spending decisions.

> Budgeting, financial planning, risk concepts.

Older adult:

> Managing financial decisions relevant to their goals.

The system should adapt without stereotyping.
